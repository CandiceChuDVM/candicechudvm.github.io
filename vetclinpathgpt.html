Educational use only VetClinPathGPT is a study aid. It does not provide diagnoses, treatment recommendations, or professional medical advice, and it must not be used to make decisions about a patient. Generative models can produce confident errors — verify everything against primary references and a qualified clinical pathologist.

What it is

A free, openly available AI tutor for veterinary clinical pathology. It works conversationally: a learner brings a question, a set of laboratory values, or a case scenario, and the tutor walks through the reasoning rather than simply returning an answer.

Who it is for

  • DVM students working through clinical pathology coursework
  • Veterinary interns consolidating laboratory interpretation on the floor
  • Residents preparing for board examinations
  • Practising veterinarians refreshing clinical pathology fundamentals

How it should be used

As an educational aid that supplements teaching, textbooks and specialist supervision — never as a diagnostic system. It is most useful for rehearsing interpretive reasoning: why an acid-base pattern fits a differential, how to build a systematic approach to a haemogram, or where a cytologic description leaves ambiguity. Learners are expected to check its output, and disagreement with the tutor is itself a productive teaching moment.

Educational research

The tutor doubles as a research instrument. Current questions concern how students actually use a conversational tutor, what usage volume reveals about study behaviour, whether the tool changes measured learning outcomes, and where its limitations show — including variation in how much learners trust generative output. Findings inform the AI literacy curriculum described on the AI Education page.

  • Usage patterns and conversation volume across a semester
  • Learning assessment alongside conventional instruction
  • Documented limitations, including hallucinated findings
  • How trust in AI output varies between learners
